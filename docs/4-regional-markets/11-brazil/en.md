# Search Engines in Brazil — Google Country and the Bing Tail

Brazil is a big, simple search market. Google above 90%, a Bing tail
that runs noticeably hotter than its global average, and no local
engine of any consequence. What makes Brazil interesting as a page in
this chapter is not competitive structure — it is that a large,
Portuguese-speaking, mobile-first market has a few rules of its own,
and that the Bing tail is real enough to matter for desktop audiences.

> **TL;DR.** 2025 referrals: Google 92.0%, Bing 5.9%, Yahoo! 1.6%.
> On desktop, Bing holds 13.5% (2025 average) and climbed toward 16%
> through 2026 — its strongest showing in any market in this chapter.
> Do Google in Brazilian Portuguese, mobile-first. Add one Bing
> account + [IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/) if desktop traffic
> matters. There is no local engine to worry about.

Google at 92%. Bing at 5.9% — almost one and a half times its ~4%
global average. Yahoo! at 1.6%. Nothing else above 0.3%, and no
Brazilian engine at all.

## The Bing tail is real here

[![Brazil — mobile vs desktop](https://x-cmd.com/draw?type=bar&unit=%25&title=Brazil%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Bing,Yahoo!&series=Desktop:82.3,13.5,3.7&series=Mobile:95.5,0.7,0.3)](https://x-cmd.com/draw/explore?type=bar&unit=%25&title=Brazil%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Bing,Yahoo!&series=Desktop:82.3,13.5,3.7&series=Mobile:95.5,0.7,0.3)

On desktop, Bing averaged 13.5% in 2025 and ran between roughly 13%
and 19% across 2026 — well above the ~11% worldwide desktop average,
though below Japan's and Germany's desktop figures. On mobile it is
under 1%, as everywhere.

The cause is the familiar one — Windows and Edge defaults, and Brazil
has a large installed base of Windows PCs in offices — and so is the
practical reading: **if your Brazilian audience sits in front of
company laptops, Bing is a much larger slice than its headline number**, and
covering it costs one [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters)
account and one IndexNow setup.

## Portuguese, not Spanish

The most common mistake about the region:
assuming the region is one language. Brazil is Portuguese — and
Brazilian Portuguese at that, which differs from European Portuguese
in vocabulary, spelling (since the 1990s orthographic agreement, in
still-noticeable ways), and idiom.

Practical consequences:

- Write **Brazilian Portuguese** (`pt-BR`), and set `hreflang`
  accordingly if you also serve Portugal (`pt-PT`). The same page
  serves both badly.
- Brazilian vocabulary matters in keywords: celular not telemóvel,
  ônibus not autocarro, trem not comboio. A European-Portuguese page
  ranks in Brazil only awkwardly, and vice versa.
- Brazil's own internet vocabulary is large and distinctive — slang,
  abbreviations and meme vocabulary that keyword tools built for
  English will not surface. Local keyword research matters here
  more than in most markets.

## Mobile-first, and then some

Brazil is one of the most mobile-heavy large markets in the world: a
substantial share of users are mobile-only, on mid-range Android
phones, frequently on prepaid data. The performance bar from
[India](https://x-cmd.com/seo/india) applies directly — test on
mid-range Android, respect the data plan, keep pages light.

Two Brazil-specific notes:

- **WhatsApp is the other half of the internet.** With over 90% reach
  among internet users, WhatsApp Business and its click-to-chat links
  are a conversion and support surface that search feeds into. It is
  not search, but it is where search traffic lands.
- **Local commerce runs through marketplaces too** — Mercado Livre and
  Shopee Brasil absorb product queries, the same dynamic as
  [Southeast Asia](https://x-cmd.com/seo/southeast-asia), though less
  extreme.

## What this means in practice

1. **Google, in Brazilian Portuguese.** Search Console, sitemap,
   structured data, mobile-first performance. Set `lang="pt-BR"`.
2. **`hreflang` between pt-BR and pt-PT** if you serve both — they are
   different markets with different vocabulary, not two spellings of
   one page.
3. **Bing Webmaster Tools + IndexNow** — one setup covers Bing (5.9%
   overall, 13.5% desktop) and its satellites.
4. **Local keyword research.** Brazilian slang and idiom do not show
   up in English-first keyword tools; use local data or local ears.
5. **Mid-range Android as the performance baseline.**
6. **WhatsApp Business** as the landing surface for commercial pages —
   click-to-chat links in titles, descriptions and structured data
   where appropriate.
7. **Links from Brazilian sites.** Local media (G1, UOL, Folha) and
   local directories are the citation layer for local queries.

## Where to go from here

Brazil is the chapter's other baseline case alongside
[India](https://x-cmd.com/seo/india) — engine choice settled, work in
the language and the device. The contrast cases are
[Korea](https://x-cmd.com/seo/korea) and [Russia](https://x-cmd.com/seo/russia),
where a local index wins the market. The Bing mechanics are in
[Bing + IndexNow](https://x-cmd.com/seo/bing-indexnow).
