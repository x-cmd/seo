# Summary

Brazil is a big, simple search market: Google above 90% on 2025 referral averages, Bing at 5.9% — almost one and a half times its global average — Yahoo! at 1.6%, and no local engine of any consequence. What earns Brazil its page is not competitive structure but three local rules. First, the Bing tail is real here: on desktop it averaged 13.5% in 2025 and ran between roughly 13% and 19% across 2026 — above the ~11% worldwide desktop average, below Japan's and Germany's figures — driven by Windows and Edge defaults plus Brazil's large office PC base. Covering it is one Webmaster Tools account and one IndexNow setup. Second, the language is Portuguese, not Spanish, and Brazilian Portuguese at that: vocabulary, spelling and idiom differ from European Portuguese (celular not telemóvel, ônibus not autocarro), so write pt-BR, hreflang against pt-PT, and expect English-first keyword tools to miss the slang and meme vocabulary that Brazilian queries actually carry. Third, the market is mobile-first to an extreme — many users are mobile-only on mid-range Android with prepaid data, so the India performance bar applies directly. Two Brazil-specific notes complete the picture: WhatsApp reaches over 90% of internet users, making WhatsApp Business and click-to-chat links the conversion surface search traffic lands on; and Mercado Livre and Shopee Brasil absorb product queries the same way Shopee and Lazada do in Southeast Asia, less extremely.
