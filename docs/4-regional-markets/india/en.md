# Search Engines in India — Google's Near-Monopoly and the AI Wobble

India is the purest Google market of its size anywhere. Above 97% of
searches — no local champion, no meaningful second engine, no wrapper
with a legacy audience. The two things that make Indian search
interesting are not engine choice at all: they are **language** (a
country searching in English and more than twenty Indic languages) and
**device** (a country that came online through the phone and often
never owned a PC). And lately, a third: the AI assistants that are
beginning to absorb queries before a search box is ever opened.

> **TL;DR.** 2025 referrals: Google 97.3%, Bing 1.3%, Yahoo! 0.5%,
> DuckDuckGo 0.7%. Do Google, in every language your audience speaks.
> The interesting questions are language mix (English versus Hindi
> versus regional languages), mobile-only performance, and voice
> search — because a large share of Indian users' first internet
> device was a phone they speak to.

Google at 97–98% month after month. Bing at 1.3%. Nothing else above
1%. There is no Indian Naver, no Indian Seznam — the domestic
champions of Indian internet (Flipkart, Zomato, Paytm, and so on) won
their categories as apps and portals, not as search engines.

The reason often given is structural: most of India came online through
inexpensive Android phones, where Google is the default search — and
in this market, unlike in Korea, no local engine had already locked in
the homepage habit before that default arrived.

## Language: the real complexity

India has 22 scheduled languages and hundreds of spoken ones. For
search, this shakes out into three buckets.

**English queries** dominate commerce and technology — anyone shopping
for a phone, comparing SaaS pricing, or reading developer documentation
is probably searching in English. If your audience is urban,
professional, or technical, an English-only site can work.

**Hindi** is the single largest language and the fastest-growing query
segment on Google in India. Hindi queries skew toward content, video,
news, government services and local commerce. Written in Devanagari,
and often transliterated into Latin script — a user may type `matlab`
or `मतलब` interchangeably, and your page needs to handle the traffic
whichever way it arrives.

**Regional languages** — Bengali, Telugu, Marathi, Tamil, Urdu,
Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, and more — each carry tens of
millions of speakers. Google's coverage of them has improved rapidly,
and the audience for each is large in absolute terms even though each
is a small slice of the query mix.

The practical rules follow directly:

- Decide your **language strategy explicitly** — which languages, on
  which pages, at which URLs — rather than letting it happen.
- Use **`hreflang`** between English, Hindi and any regional versions,
  with the correct region codes (`en-IN`, `hi`, `ta`, and so on).
- Do not machine-translate. Indic-language users notice, and so does
  Google's evaluation of page quality.
- For **transliterated queries**, make sure your titles and headings
  carry the common Latin-script spellings as well as the native script,
  where that reads naturally.

## Device: mobile-only, and often slow

India is a mobile-first internet market in the strict sense: for a
large share of users, the phone is not the primary device but the only
device, sometimes on a modest data plan and a low-end Android phone.

That makes the standard advice *more* true, not less:

- **Core Web Vitals on low-end Android over slow connections** is the
  realistic bar, not a fast phone on wifi.
- **Light pages win.** Every megabyte costs a share of your audience.
- **AMP is no longer the answer it once seemed** — Google retired most
  of AMP's search advantages — but the discipline it enforced (small,
  fast pages) is still exactly right.
- **Voice search** matters more here than in most markets. A spoken
  Hindi or Tamil query is longer and more conversational than a typed
  one, and the pages that answer conversational, question-shaped
  queries are the ones that capture it.

## The AI wobble

The most recent change in Indian search is not an engine taking share
from Google — it is **AI products taking queries from search itself**.

Google's own AI Overviews roll out in India across English and Hindi.
ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity have large and growing Indian user
bases; India has consistently been one of ChatGPT's largest markets.
And vernacular AI assistants (including voice-first products built
for Indian languages) reach users for whom typing was always the
barrier.

What this does to an optimizer:

- **The "position zero" surfaces are multiplying.** An AI Overview, a
  chat answer, and a classic featured snippet can all sit above the
  organic results for the same query.
- **Being the cited source matters more than ranking #2.** AI answers
  cite a handful of pages; being one of them is worth more than a blue
  link at position two.
- **Question-shaped content wins the voice and AI layer.** Pages that
  answer a real question in the first paragraph, with clear structure
  and genuine expertise, are the ones both algorithms and language
  models can use.

None of this changes the engine choice — it is still Google. It
changes what a page has to look like to win.

## What to actually do

1. **Google, in every language your audience actually speaks.** Be
   deliberate about the language set; `hreflang` between versions.
2. **Design for a low-end Android phone on a slow connection.** Test
   performance there, not on your laptop.
3. **Write question-shaped answers** in the opening of important
   pages — for voice search and for AI citation.
4. **Bing costs one account** — [Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters)
   plus [IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/) — and covers 1.3% plus
   its satellites. Worth it, low priority.
5. **Watch the AI surfaces** in your own analytics: AI Overview
   citations and chat-app referrals show up as distinct traffic
   patterns.

## Where to go from here

India is the chapter's baseline case — what a market looks like when
engine choice is settled and everything interesting is language,
device and AI. The contrast cases are [Korea](https://x-cmd.com/seo/korea)
and [Russia](https://x-cmd.com/seo/russia), where a local index wins.
[southeast-asia](https://x-cmd.com/seo/southeast-asia) shares India's
mobile-first, app-heavy shape with even more in-app search.
