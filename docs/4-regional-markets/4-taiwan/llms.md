# Core Content

core_features:
  - Google holds about four-fifths of Taiwanese search; Yahoo! Taiwan holds an unusually large second place but serves organic results from Google's own index
  - Consequence: no Yahoo! Taiwan webmaster tool, no separate index, no second link profile — Google work carries over, as in Japan
  - Desktop is where the competition lives: Google 65.7%, Yahoo! 17.1%, Bing 16.7%; mobile is 87.6% Google, 11.3% Yahoo!, 0.4% Bing
  - Bing's share is concentrated on desktop; on mobile it is under 1% — one Bing Webmaster Tools account plus IndexNow
  - Traditional Chinese (zh-TW) and Simplified Chinese (zh-CN) are different queries, not variants of one query; a page written for one audience is nearly invisible to the other
  - Google's script-conversion matching is a fallback, not a strategy — a Simplified page surfaced for a Traditional query reads as foreign
  - Taiwan vocabulary differs from mainland vocabulary (網路/网络, 軟體/软件, 影片/视频); using the wrong word reads as machine translation
  - Baidu's share in Taiwan is negligible — it indexes the mainland web, not Taiwan's, and belongs in a China strategy, not a Taiwan one
  - Local search runs on Google Business Profile plus Taiwanese directories with NAP consistency
  - LINE is the dominant messenger in Taiwan; many Taiwanese businesses get more from a LINE Official Account than from another round of on-page work

# Summary

Taiwan is a Google market with a twist: Google holds about 80% of searches and Yahoo! Taiwan an unusually large 13% second place — but Yahoo! Taiwan serves organic results from Google's own index, so the engine question is easy and no separate strategy, tool or link profile is needed. The device split is where the competition actually lives: on desktop Google drops to 65.7% while Yahoo! Taiwan takes 17.1% and Bing 16.7%, whereas mobile is 87.6% Google — so Bing coverage corresponds to the desktop slice only. The genuinely hard part of Taiwan is that Chinese comes in two written forms and the engines treat them as different queries: a user searching 網路 wants pages written in Traditional characters with Taiwan's own vocabulary (軟體 not 软件, 影片 not 视频), and a Simplified page surfaced via Google's script conversion reads as foreign to the person who lands on it. The practical checklist is therefore linguistic rather than algorithmic: declare zh-TW, write Traditional characters into titles, descriptions, alt text and structured data, use Taiwan's vocabulary rather than a translation, localize prices and addresses, set hreflang explicitly across language versions, and leave Baidu out — it indexes the mainland web and belongs in a China strategy. Local search runs on Google Business Profile with consistent citations across Taiwanese directories, and LINE — far more dominant in Taiwan than in most markets — makes an Official Account, for many businesses, more productive than another round of on-page work.
