# Search Engines in Southeast Asia — Google Defaults and In-App Search

Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines look, on a share chart, like
the easiest markets in this chapter: Google everywhere, nothing else
above 5%. Two things make them more interesting than the chart
suggests. First, *why* Google's share is so high — the Android default,
in markets where almost everyone's first device was an Android phone.
Second, *where the missing queries went* — into app-internal search:
TikTok, Instagram, Shopee, Lazada, Grab. A strategy that only covers
the search box covers a shrinking slice of how these markets actually
look things up.

> **TL;DR.** 2025 referrals — Indonesia: Google 93.0%, Bing 2.5%,
> Yahoo! 2.1%. Thailand: Google 97.8%, Bing 1.4%. Philippines:
> Google 90.9%, Bing 5.7%, Yahoo! 2.9%. Do Google, in the local
> language, mobile-first. Then accept that a growing share of
> "search" happens inside apps, and decide deliberately whether that
> traffic is yours to win.

Google between 91% and 98% across all three. The Philippines is the
one place in this chapter where Bing clears 5% — its audience skews
to office desktops, same as everywhere. And Yahoo! still holds 2–3%
in Indonesia and the Philippines, its last real strongholds anywhere
— a legacy of the portal era, older users, and Yahoo mail habits.

None of these numbers hide a local engine. There is no Indonesian or
Thai or Filipino Naver. The local champions of Southeast Asian internet
— Shopee, Lazada, Grab, GoTo, Tokopedia — are marketplaces and
super-apps, and their search boxes live inside their apps.

## Why Google's number is so high

Android. Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines are overwhelmingly
Android markets — the iPhone is a minority device — and Google Search
is what the Android home screen search bar and Chrome default to.
When almost an entire country's first internet device is an Android
phone, the default search inherits the country.

This is worth stating precisely because it cuts both ways. It means
Google's position here is less a *choice* than a *setting* — the same
fact that changed the policy debate in
[Japan](https://x-cmd.com/seo/japan). If defaults are ever contested in
these markets the way Japan's smartphone law now contests them, the
floor moves. Nothing suggests that is imminent; everything about the
share charts says it is the thing to watch.

## Where the queries actually go

The share chart counts referrals from search engines. It does not
count the searches that never touch one.

**TikTok as a search engine.** For a large share of younger users in
all three markets, TikTok's search bar is where queries about food,
travel, products, "how to" and "is it worth it" actually start. The
results are videos, the ranking signals are engagement, and your
Google optimization does nothing there. If your product demos well on
video, TikTok SEO (keyword-rich captions, spoken keywords, hashtag
discipline) is a real channel with real competition.

**Marketplace search.** Shopee, Lazada and Tokopedia are where product
queries with buying intent go. Their in-app search has its own
ranking factors (sales velocity, reviews, listing completeness,
sponsored placement) and its own optimization discipline. For an
e-commerce business, marketplace SEO often delivers more than web SEO
in this region.

**Super-app discovery.** Grab, Gojek and LINE (Thailand's dominant
messenger, with its own services and official accounts) sit on local
intent — food, transport, delivery, local services — that never
becomes a web query.

The honest way to hold this: the search engine is still the biggest
single surface, and Google is nearly all of it. But "search behavior"
in these markets is broader than the search box, and a plan that never
asks about the app layer is a plan with a hole in it.

## Local portals still have a role

Each market has legacy portals that still draw real traffic —
Detik and Kompas in Indonesia, Sanook and Kapook in Thailand — and
they matter mostly as *link sources and citation surfaces* rather than
as engines. A mention or a link from a dominant local portal is one of
the strongest local signals you can get, for Google in that market,
even though nobody searches on the portal's own engine.

## What to actually do

1. **Google, in the local language, mobile-first.** Bahasa Indonesia
   for Indonesia, Thai for Thailand (and note that Thai has no spaces
   between words — it changes how keyword research and headings work),
   Filipino/English for the Philippines, where English content is
   fully viable.
2. **Test performance on low-end Android.** Same bar as
   [India](https://x-cmd.com/seo/india): modest hardware, real network
   conditions.
3. **One Bing account + IndexNow.** Covers the 2–6% and its
   satellites; low effort, low priority.
4. **Decide deliberately about app-layer search.** If you sell
   products, marketplace SEO (Shopee/Lazada/Tokopedia) is probably
   worth more than another month of on-page work. If your product is
   visual, TikTok search is a real channel.
5. **Get links from the local portals.** Detik, Kompas, Sanook, and
   local news — the strongest local citations for Google in these
   markets.
6. **LINE, for Thailand.** Official accounts and LINE TODAY are a
   discovery surface with no web equivalent.

## Where to go from here

Southeast Asia pairs with [India](https://x-cmd.com/seo/india) — the
same mobile-first, default-driven, app-heavy shape — and contrasts
with [Vietnam](https://x-cmd.com/seo/vietnam), the one market in the
region with its own national index. The mechanics of the Bing side
are in [Bing + IndexNow](https://x-cmd.com/seo/bing-indexnow); the
measurement caveats are in the
[regional overview](https://x-cmd.com/seo/overview).
