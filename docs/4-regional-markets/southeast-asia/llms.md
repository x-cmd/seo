# Summary

Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines look like the easiest markets in this chapter — Google between 91% and 98%, nothing else above 6% — but the chart hides two things. First, why the share is so high: these are overwhelmingly Android markets where almost everyone's first internet device was an Android phone, and the default search inherits the country. Google's position here is a setting more than a choice, and the Japan smartphone-law precedent is the thing to watch if defaults are ever contested. Second, where the missing queries went: into app-internal search. TikTok's search bar is where younger users start queries about food, travel, products and "is it worth it" — video results ranked by engagement, untouched by Google optimization. Shopee, Lazada and Tokopedia absorb product queries with buying intent and have their own ranking factors (sales velocity, reviews, listing completeness, sponsored placement), so marketplace SEO often out-delivers web SEO for e-commerce in this region. Grab, Gojek and LINE (dominant in Thailand, with official accounts and LINE TODAY) absorb local intent that never becomes a web query. Yahoo! still holds 2–3% in Indonesia and the Philippines, its last real strongholds, and the Philippines is the one chapter market where Bing clears 5% on office desktops. Local portals — Detik and Kompas, Sanook and Kapook — matter not as engines but as the strongest local link sources and citations for Google in these markets. The practical program: Google in the local language and mobile-first (Bahasa Indonesia; Thai, noting it has no spaces between words; English is fully viable in the Philippines), performance tested on low-end Android, one Bing account plus IndexNow for the tail, a deliberate decision about the app layer, portal links, and a LINE Official Account for Thailand.
