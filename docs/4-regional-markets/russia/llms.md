# Summary

Russia is the only very large market where Google is not the leading search engine: Yandex holds roughly three-quarters of searches on 2025 referral averages (73.2% against Google's 25.1%), and the shape has been stable for years. The victory follows the same three-part pattern as Naver in Korea and Seznam in Czechia, at larger scale — an own index crawled by YandexBot (matched only by Google, Bing and Baidu), with ranking signals that weigh Russian-language content and behavior heavily, so Google rankings carry over imperfectly; a product ecosystem (Maps, Mail, Disk, Market, Zen, Direct, Metrica, Alice, ride-hailing under one account) that locks in the homepage habit; and full tooling, where Yandex.Webmaster is a Search Console equivalent and Yandex.Metrica a free analytics suite. Yandex is also a founding IndexNow partner, so instant submission covers Yandex, Bing and Seznam from one endpoint. Google weakened in Russia after 2022 as the company slowed Russia-facing operations and users and advertisers moved, but it remains the clear second engine, and a quarter of the market is enormous in absolute terms. The parts of a Russian launch that actually fail have nothing to do with keywords: international payment cards do not work normally, which makes buying Yandex Direct ads from abroad difficult or impossible; some hosts and CDNs will not serve Russian traffic, and performance is a ranking signal everywhere including Yandex; and Russian data-localization law requires citizens' personal data on servers physically in Russia — Metrica is compliant by design, and anything homegrown is a question for legal, not SEO. The workflow itself is familiar: verify in Yandex.Webmaster, submit the sitemap, enable IndexNow, write Russian for Russians, and check hosting performance from inside Russia before scaling.
