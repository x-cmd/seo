# Summary

Germany, France and the Netherlands look like Google markets with a strong second player, but read the numbers before assuming the second player is local. On 2025 referral averages Google holds 86.6% in Germany, 88.0% in France and 88.5% in the Netherlands; the runner-up everywhere is Bing at 4.9–6.4%, and the beloved local privacy engines together hold only 1–2%. Bing's real size is on the desktop — 15.8% in Germany on the 2025 average and still climbing through 2026 to around 19.7%, against under 1% on mobile — which matters mainly for B2B audiences on work laptops and costs one Bing Webmaster Tools account plus IndexNow. The privacy cluster is genuinely popular as brands and tiny as share: Ecosia (Berlin, runs on Bing's index, funds tree-planting), Startpage (Dutch, proxies Google results from EU servers), Qwant (French, EU-hosted, own index since leaving Bing) and MetaGer (German meta-search). Two of the four ride indexes you already cover, so optimizing for them is literally the same pages ranked by somebody else's index; the other two are under 1% each and the return on a dedicated strategy rounds to zero. They are worth reading as a weather vane — a measurable slice of these users chooses privacy-first when the choice is easy, and Ecosia's share spikes whenever a Google privacy story trends. The dramatic "Google fell to 60% in Germany" claims are usually desktop-only slices of particular months; the accurate statement is that desktop Bing in German-speaking Europe has been grinding upward for years, which is a reason to keep Bing coverage healthy, not a reason to treat Bing as a different index. The remaining work is language: German for the DACH countries with hreflang between de-DE, de-AT and de-CH, and vocabulary that reads as native where the audience lives.
