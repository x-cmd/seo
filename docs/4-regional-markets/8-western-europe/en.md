# Search Engines in Western Europe — Google, Bing and the Privacy Cluster

Germany, France and the Netherlands look like Google markets with a
strong second player — but read the numbers before assuming the second
player is local. In Germany the runner-up is Bing at over 6%, and the
beloved local privacy engines — Ecosia, Startpage, Qwant, MetaGer —
together hold only 1–2%. This page covers what the numbers actually
say, what the privacy cluster means, and what (little) changes in your
work because of it.

> **TL;DR.** 2025 referrals — Germany: Google 86.6%, Bing 6.4%,
> Yahoo! 1.6%, DuckDuckGo 1.1%–1.5%, Ecosia 1.1%. France: Google
> 88.0%, Bing 5.4%, Ecosia 1.2%, Qwant 0.9%. Netherlands: Google
> 88.5%, Bing 4.9%. On **desktop** Bing is far bigger — 15.8% in
> Germany on the 2025 average, and between roughly 13% and 18% across
> 2026. The local privacy engines run on indexes that other engines
> already cover: Ecosia uses Bing's, Startpage proxies Google's.

## Bing's real size is on the desktop

[![Germany — mobile vs desktop](https://x-cmd.com/draw?type=bar&unit=%25&title=Germany%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Bing,Yahoo!,DuckDuckGo,Ecosia&series=Mobile:93.4,0.8,0.3,1.1,1.1&series=Desktop:74.8,15.8,3.7,2.2,1.1)](https://x-cmd.com/draw/explore?type=bar&unit=%25&title=Germany%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Bing,Yahoo!,DuckDuckGo,Ecosia&series=Mobile:93.4,0.8,0.3,1.1,1.1&series=Desktop:74.8,15.8,3.7,2.2,1.1)

On German desktops Bing holds 15.8% (2025 average). Through 2026 the
number moved in a band — about 18% early in the year, easing to around
13% by August — and then jumped to nearly 20% in September and October.
That last jump deserves suspicion: StatCounter's panel shifted
worldwide in exactly those two months (the same break shows up in
every country's series), so the late-2026 readings are not comparable
with what came before. On phones Bing is under 1% throughout. The
pattern behind the whole curve is the Windows/Edge default, and it
repeats in France and the Netherlands.

What the desktop number means in practice: for audiences that sit in
front of work laptops — B2B software, SaaS, anything sold to offices —
Bing is a much bigger slice than the 6% all-device headline suggests,
while for phone audiences it barely exists. Covering it is one
[Bing Webmaster Tools](https://www.bing.com/webmasters) account and
[IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/), the same setup described in
[Bing + IndexNow](https://x-cmd.com/seo/bing-indexnow).

## The privacy cluster — what it is and what it is worth

These three countries have a set of home-grown engines with a privacy
or values story. They are genuinely popular as *brands*; they are tiny
as *market share*.

| Engine | Country | How it searches | 2025 share |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| **[Ecosia](https://www.ecosia.org/)** | Germany (Berlin) | Runs on Bing's index; ad revenue funds tree-planting | ~1.1% DE, 1.2% FR |
| **[Startpage](https://www.startpage.com/)** | Netherlands | Proxies Google results from EU servers | <1% NL |
| **[Qwant](https://www.qwant.com/)** | France | EU-hosted; built its own index after leaving Bing behind | ~0.9% FR |
| **[MetaGer](https://metager.org/)** | Germany (Hannover) | Meta-search — merges results from multiple engines | <0.5% DE |

Why they matter despite the size:

**Two of the four ride indexes you already cover.** Ecosia serves
Bing's index, Startpage serves Google's. Optimizing for them is not a
separate task; it is literally the same pages, ranked by somebody
else's index. Qwant and MetaGer are the exceptions — Qwant maintains
its own index, and MetaGer is a meta-search — but at under 1% each,
the return on a dedicated strategy rounds to zero.

**They are a weather vane.** The share is small, but the *preference*
they express is real: a measurable slice of German, French and Dutch
users choose a privacy-first engine when the choice is easy. That is
worth knowing when you decide what tracking and consent notices to put
on your EU pages, and it occasionally spikes — Ecosia's share jumps
whenever a Google privacy story trends.

## Should Google's slide change anything?

You will sometimes read that Google's German share has fallen
dramatically — from ~90% toward ~60%. Check the source and the device
before repeating that. On the 2025 all-device referral series, Google
Germany averaged 86.6%, with desktop at ~74.8% and mobile at ~93.4%.
The lower numbers people quote are usually desktop-only slices of
particular months, where Bing's Windows default is strongest — a real
trend on one device class, not a collapse.

The accurate statement is narrower: **desktop Bing in Germany runs
ten points or more above its all-device headline** (15.8% against
6.4% in 2025), and that gap is a real reason to keep Bing coverage
healthy. It is not a reason to build a German Bing strategy as if
Bing were a different index — it is the same work, one more sitemap
submission.

## What this means in practice

1. **Google, in the local language.** Search Console, sitemap,
   structured data, Core Web Vitals — German for Germany/Austria/
   Switzerland, French for France, Dutch for the Netherlands.
   `hreflang` between de-DE / de-AT / de-CH if you serve them all.
2. **Bing Webmaster Tools + IndexNow.** One setup covers Bing, and
   through Bing's index also DuckDuckGo, Yahoo! and Ecosia.
3. **No separate work for the privacy engines.** Ecosia and Startpage
   already serve your pages if Bing and Google have them. Qwant and
   MetaGer are too small to justify a strategy; submit to Qwant only
   if French traffic is a priority and the cost is one form.
4. **Dialect and vocabulary care.** German varies meaningfully between
   Germany, Austria and Switzerland (Kartoffel vs Erdapfel is the
   classic example, but commercial vocabulary varies too). The same
   page can rank in all three, but the language should read as native
   where the audience is.
5. **Watch desktop-versus-mobile in your analytics.** A B2B site will
   see a bigger Bing slice than 6%; a consumer mobile app will see
   none. Let your own data decide how much Bing attention is worth.
