# Search Engines in Japan — Yahoo! JAPAN, Google and the Rules That Changed

Japan looks like a two-horse race on paper — Google in front, Yahoo!
JAPAN second — but it is really a one-horse race wearing two uniforms.
Yahoo! JAPAN serves results from Google's index. Your Google ranking is
already your Yahoo! JAPAN ranking. What is different is everything
around the result: the interface, the vertical services stacked on top,
and, as of December 2025, the law that decides which search engine a
phone is allowed to start with.

> **TL;DR.** 2025 referrals: Google 79.5%, Yahoo! JAPAN 9.4%, Bing 9.2%.
> Yahoo! JAPAN runs on Google's index, so **you do not need a second
> SEO strategy** — but its layout is different, so titles and click
> -through rates are not. Bing's share is almost entirely desktop
> (19.0% desktop vs 0.7% mobile). The
> [Mobile Software Competition Act](https://www.jftc.go.jp/en/pressreleases/yearly-2025/March/250331.html)
> took force in December 2025 and now lets users pick a default search
> engine on both iOS and Android — and the JFTC has already ordered
> Google to stop its Android preinstall practices.

Google at roughly four-fifths, and then a long tail of engines that
each hold under 1% except the two that matter. Yahoo! JAPAN at 9.4%
and Bing at 9.2% are a tie, and for entirely different reasons.

Bing is here because of Windows. Yahoo! JAPAN is here because it has
been the default on Japanese phones and a habit for two decades.

## Yahoo! JAPAN is a wrapper, not a competitor

This is the single most useful fact about Japanese search, and it is
unlike anything in Korea or the Czech Republic.

Since 2010, Yahoo! JAPAN has used Google's search index for its
organic results, under a partnership that the Japanese regulator
approved. It is not a metasearch — it does not query other engines and
merge the answers — it is Google's index with a different product
wrapped around it. Google was even paid for the arrangement, and
Microsoft publicly objected to it as anticompetitive at the time.

What Yahoo! JAPAN adds on top is substantial:

- **Its own interface.** A results page that looks nothing like
  Google's, with a different layout, different ordering cues, and
  different truncation of titles and descriptions.
- **Its own verticals.** Weather through !-spotlight, news, shopping,
  maps, dictionaries, timetables, cloud storage, e-books, video, and
  the PayPay wallet. These sit on and around the organic results and
  capture a lot of the search real estate.
- **Its own ranking layer.** Ordering is not byte-identical to
  Google's, so a page that ranks 4th on Google may not be in the same
  position on Yahoo! JAPAN.

**The practical consequence is good news.** You do not need a Japanese
Yahoo! strategy. You need a Google strategy, done with Japanese
keywords, and then you need to check how your pages look and click in
Yahoo! JAPAN's layout. That is a title-tag and meta-description
discipline job, not a link-building campaign.

There is no Yahoo! JAPAN webmaster tool to submit to, because there is
no separate index to submit to. If you want to be in Japanese search
beyond Google, the adjacent move is Bing — Bing Webmaster Tools and
[IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/) — which covers Bing's 9.2%.

## Bing is a desktop engine here, Yahoo! JAPAN is a mobile one

[![Japan — mobile vs desktop](https://x-cmd.com/draw?type=bar&unit=%25&title=Japan%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Yahoo!,Bing&series=Mobile:85.0,12.2,0.7&series=Desktop:73.2,6.1,19.0)](https://x-cmd.com/draw/explore?type=bar&unit=%25&title=Japan%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Yahoo!,Bing&series=Mobile:85.0,12.2,0.7&series=Desktop:73.2,6.1,19.0)

On a phone, Bing is essentially absent — 0.7%. On a desktop it holds
19%, three times Yahoo! JAPAN's share there. The reason is the same
one that puts Bing at 5% in Germany and 6% in Brazil: it is what
Windows and Edge are wired to do, and desktops are where those run.

So the two non-Google engines in Japan cover opposite ends of the
device spectrum. If your traffic is mobile, Yahoo! JAPAN is the one
that matters, and it is mostly free for you. If your traffic is
desktop, Bing is worth the small effort of a Bing Webmaster Tools
account.

## The December 2025 rule change

Japan's **Mobile Software Competition Act** (Act on Promotion of
Competition for Specified Smartphone Software) came into force in
December 2025, and it changed the single biggest lever in Japanese
search: the default search engine on a phone.

What it did:

- **iOS.** Apple shipped a Safari search-engine choice screen in
  Japan with iOS 26.2 in November 2025, ahead of the law. Japanese
  iPhone users can now pick their default search engine on first
  setup.
- **Android.** Google had to add a choice screen for browser and
  search-app selection, so a default is no longer simply preinstalled.
- **Enforcement.** The Japan Fair Trade Commission issued its first
  compliance reports under the Act and ordered Google to stop
  practices that put rivals at a disadvantage on Android
  preinstallation and search defaults.

Why this matters for SEO: for years, a large share of Japanese mobile
search was not a *choice* at all — it was a setting. The single most
valuable default in mobile search is now, in principle, contestable.

That does not mean Google is about to lose Japan. It means the floor
under it is now a policy question rather than a technical given, and
that the search bar in a Japanese phone is a place where another
engine can now be chosen. If you are not Google, this is the part of
the Japanese market worth watching.

## The carrier portals are gone

Japanese search used to be dotted with carrier-branded engines:
**goo** (KDDI/au), **BIGLOBE** (SoftBank) and **OCN** (NTT Docomo),
each with its own search front end.

That era is ending. The goo portal service ended in November 2025, and
the carrier search products have largely converged into redirects
toward Yahoo! JAPAN or Google. Practically, "optimize for the carrier
engine" is no longer a task in Japan — there is no separate index
there to be found in.

## Yahoo! JAPAN is hedging on Google

The Google arrangement has been in place since 2010 and has been
renewed many times, but LINE Yahoo (the merged LINE and Yahoo Japan
company, LY Corporation) has publicly signalled that it wants to
reduce its dependence on it. There has been reporting on testing an
alternative organic backend, and on the possibility of using an index
from another operator — including Naver's — for some queries.

Nothing about this has changed the market yet. But if it did, Japan
would become a market where a *foreign* engine with its *own* index
held a double-digit share — a genuinely new competitive shape, and
worth re-reading this page when it happens.

## What to actually do

**If you are launching in Japan**, in order:

1. **Google, in Japanese.** Search Console, sitemap, structured data,
   mobile-first performance. Japanese users are overwhelmingly on
   phones, so mobile Core Web Vitals are the thing to get right.
2. **Write the titles for Yahoo! JAPAN's layout too.** Same content,
   different frame — check how your title and description render and
   whether they earn the click in Yahoo! JAPAN's denser layout.
3. **Add Bing.** 9.2% overall and 19% on desktop, and one
   [IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/) submission covers it.
4. **Do not chase the carrier engines.** goo, BIGLOBE and OCN search
   no longer have their own index.
5. **Get Japanese-language links.** For the local-language queries
   that matter, a link from a Japanese site is worth more than the
   same link from abroad.
6. **Watch the default-search rules.** If the choice screen starts
   moving volume away from Google, that changes the arithmetic for
   every Japanese campaign.

**If you are not launching in Japan**, the transferable lesson is
narrow but real: not every second-place engine needs its own strategy.
Check whether the engine you are worried about has its own index
before you build one.
