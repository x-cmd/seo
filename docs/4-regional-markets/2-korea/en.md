# Search Engines in Korea — Naver, Google, Daum and KakaoTalk

Korea is the one market where a local search engine genuinely beats
Google, and the reason is not that Koreans dislike Google. It is that
Naver answers a question the way a Korean person asks it — in whole
sentences, about local things, with the answer assembled from Naver's
own blogs, cafes, maps and Q&A. If your site is only optimized for
Google, roughly a third of Korean search demand is simply out of reach.

> **TL;DR.** On 2025 query volume, Naver 62.9% / Google 29.6%. On 2025
> outbound referrals, Google 45.8% / Naver 45.6% — nearly tied. Both
> numbers are true; they count different things. Naver is a **mobile**
> engine (60.8% of its share) and Google is a **desktop** engine (58.6%).
> Naver ranks its own verticals — blog, cafe, place, Knowledge iN — with
> its own signals (C-Rank, D.I.A.), so it needs its own strategy.
> Daum is down to ~1–3% and lives inside KakaoTalk. Naver's own tool is
> [Naver Search Advisor](https://searchadvisor.naver.com/).

## What is the market actually like?

[StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/south-korea)
monthly averages:

Two engines hold nearly everything, and the third (Bing at 5%) is there
because it ships as the Windows default rather than because anyone
chose it. Everything below that — Cốc Cốc, Yandex, Yahoo! — is under 1%
each and only matters if you already have a reason to care.

### Why you will find two different answers for Korea

Search for "Korean search engine market share" and you will find a
figure of 52.4% for Google and 38.2% for Naver floating around on
blogs and aggregator sites. That particular pair does not match
either of the two major trackers, in either direction, for any device
split. Treat it as unverified and move on.

What you will also find is this, and this is the real picture:

[![South Korea — two ways to count](https://x-cmd.com/draw?type=bar&unit=%25&title=South%20Korea%20-%20two%20ways%20to%20count&labels=Google,Naver,Bing,Daum&series=InternetTrend%20%28queries%29:29.6,62.9,3.1,2.9&series=StatCounter%20%28referrals%29:45.8,45.6,5.0,1.2)](https://x-cmd.com/draw/explore?type=bar&unit=%25&title=South%20Korea%20-%20two%20ways%20to%20count&labels=Google,Naver,Bing,Daum&series=InternetTrend%20%28queries%29:29.6,62.9,3.1,2.9&series=StatCounter%20%28referrals%29:45.8,45.6,5.0,1.2)

**InternetTrend** counts queries — every search a Korean person types.
**StatCounter** counts referrals — the clicks that actually leave the
engine for a website.

Naver scores 62.9% on the first and 45.6% on the second. The 17-point
gap is the whole story: **a large share of Korean searches are answered
inside Naver without the user ever clicking through.** Naver's own
Knowledge iN answers, its blog results, its cafe threads, its place
listings — a Korean user can finish the task on Naver and never see
your website, so no referral is recorded.

Which number should you use? If you are asking *"how many searches could
I win"*, use the query figure — that is the size of the opportunity.
If you are asking *"how many visitors could I get"*, use the referral
figure, or better, your own analytics.

The gap also tells you where to spend effort. Naver's on-platform
answers are exactly the queries that never produce a click — which
means a Naver strategy is not "rank higher on the same page", it is
"get into the vertical that answers this kind of question".

## Naver is a mobile engine, Google is a desktop engine

This is the second thing the raw numbers tell you, and it is easy to
miss:

[![South Korea — mobile vs desktop](https://x-cmd.com/draw?type=bar&unit=%25&title=South%20Korea%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Naver,Bing,Daum&series=Mobile:35.9,60.8,0.4,0.0&series=Desktop:58.6,26.1,10.8,2.8)](https://x-cmd.com/draw/explore?type=bar&unit=%25&title=South%20Korea%20-%20mobile%20vs%20desktop&labels=Google,Naver,Bing,Daum&series=Mobile:35.9,60.8,0.4,0.0&series=Desktop:58.6,26.1,10.8,2.8)

On a phone, Naver takes nearly two-thirds of searches and Google falls
to about a third. On a desktop, Google takes close to six in ten and
Naver drops to about a quarter. Daum barely registers on mobile at
all — it is a desktop portal that survives on old-PC habits.

The practical reading: **on mobile you need Naver, on desktop Google is
still the bigger prize.** A Korean launch that optimizes only for
Google is optimizing for the smaller half of the market.

## How Naver actually ranks things

Naver is not a list of blue links. It is an **integrated search**
portal that assembles a result page from its own verticals, and each
vertical has its own audience and its own signals.

| Vertical | What it is | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **VIEW** | Blends Naver **Blog** and Naver **Cafe** results | The main target for content marketing |
| **Knowledge iN (지식iN)** | Q&A platform, live since 2002 | Often sits at the top of the page for long-tail questions |
| **Naver Blog** | User blogs — reviews, long posts, photos | Carries the **C-Rank** signal |
| **Naver Cafe** | Community forums — apartments, hobbies, parenting | Frequently outranks a brand's own shopping listing |
| **Naver Place (플레이스)** | Local business listings, like Google Business Profile | Effectively mandatory for any local presence |
| **Naver Shopping** | Commerce results and Smart Store | Where the transactions are |
| **Naver News / TV / VIBE** | Syndicated media and video | The top-of-funnel layer |

Two ranking systems matter if you are serious. **C-Rank** scores a
blog's authority and how focused it is on its topic. **D.I.A.** scores
an individual piece of content — relevance, dwell time, comments,
shares. Naver's own team calls the result a closed ecosystem
(폐쇄형 생태계): much of this content cannot be crawled by Google at
all, so being absent from it is a permanent loss, not a ranking
penalty.

For a commerce query, the order is roughly: influencer reviews →
long-form blog posts (Power Contents) → Naver Place → cafe threads →
Knowledge iN → the shopping listing. A brand that shows up in three of
those blocks at once is doing better than a brand that tops one.

Two cautions. **Knowledge iN punishes manipulation** — self-answered
questions, sockpuppet accounts and bulk operations get flagged, and
getting flagged can burn an entire account matrix. Treat it as
credibility, not traffic farming. **Paid posting in a cafe violates
Korean competition rules** and the cafe terms of service; the
compliant route is seeding genuine user reviews from existing customers.

## Daum and KakaoTalk — the second ecosystem

Daum was Korea's first portal, and it was once the largest search
engine in the country. It is a different story now.

Kakao acquired Daum in 2014 and folded it into KakaoTalk. Daum's share
fell from roughly 20–30% around that merger to 1–3% today — Bing
passed it in July 2024. In December 2025 Kakao spun Daum off as AXZ;
Upstage acquired it on 7 May 2026 and renamed it Daum Corporation in
July 2026, pairing it with the SOLAR language model.

What survives is not the search box. It is the **placement inside
KakaoTalk**. Typing `#` in a chat window returns Daum results, and
KakaoTalk reaches roughly 90–98% penetration in Korea. Kakao is also
testing an AI-generated answer mode in chat that replaces the
Daum-backed `#` search entirely, so a user never leaves the app.

That makes KakaoTalk a distribution channel, not a search engine to
optimize. If your audience is Korean, a KakaoTalk channel is often a
higher-leverage move than another round of on-page SEO.

## Where Google still wins in Korea

Do not over-rotate. Even in the most Google-friendly market outside the
US, Google is strong in specific and predictable places — roughly 95%
on developer and technical queries, 88% on English and global content,
98% on YouTube-style video, 78% on academic search, and 70% on B2B
SaaS review queries. It is weakest on Korean local commerce, where it
sits near 18%.

So the split is roughly: **English, technical, academic and video
search stays Google's; Korean-language, local, consumer and commerce
search is Naver's.**

## The AI turn is moving both numbers

Two things happened in 2025 and 2026 that make any share figure older
than a year stale.

**Naver's AI Briefing** — an AI-generated summary module — rolled out
across health, public-sector and finance verticals during 2025, drawing
on trusted Korean institutions such as teaching hospitals and academic
societies. By April 2026 it appeared on roughly 20% of Naver queries
with 30 million-plus monthly users. That is a direct attack on the
"answer on the page" behaviour that made Naver's referral rate look
low.

**Google's Korean mobile app passed Naver's** for the first time in
July 2026 — 47.02 million monthly users against Naver's 46.85 million.
ChatGPT, meanwhile, reached 16.46 million monthly users in Korea,
comfortably ahead of Daum's 6.31 million. A 2025 government survey
found that 60% of generative-AI users had shifted at least half of
their conventional search activity to AI platforms.

The direction of travel is that part of the search market is moving
from "search result pages" to "AI answers" — and the engine that
wins there may not be the one that wins the traditional results.

## What to actually do

**If you are launching in Korea**, this is the order that works.

1. **Google first, always.** It is still roughly half of all outbound
   search traffic, and half of desktop. Do the ordinary work: Search
   Console, sitemap, structured data, Core Web Vitals.
2. **Add Bing.** At 5% it is not a rounding error, and Bing Webmaster
   Tools plus [IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/) costs almost
   nothing extra.
3. **Verify in Naver Search Advisor.** It is the closest thing Korea
   has to Google Search Console: site verification, crawl and index
   reports, URL inspection, and a reindex request. It governs the
   external-web layer only.
4. **Then build inside Naver.** Blog for C-Rank and content reach, Place
   for anything local, Shopping for commerce, Knowledge iN where you
   genuinely have experts to answer from.
5. **Write Korean, as a local would ask.** Korean queries are
   sentences. "서울 맛집" (Seoul restaurants) is a place plus a
   category, not a keyword string.
6. **Get links from Korean sites.** For a Naver ranking, a Korean
   blog or news link beats an equivalent international link by a wide
   margin.
7. **Watch the AI surfaces.** Naver's AI Briefing and Google's AI
   Overviews both pull from pages with clear, first-hand, citable
   content. Being the source that gets summarized is worth more than
   one more blue link.

**If you are not launching in Korea**, this page is still worth
reading once, because the pattern repeats: a local engine wins not by
out-ranking Google but by answering a different question better.
