# Core Content

core_features:
  - Two engines hold nearly all of Korea: Naver and Google, with Bing at 5% from the Windows default
  - The share depends entirely on the measurement basis — InternetTrend counts queries, StatCounter counts outbound referrals
  - Naver is 62.9% of queries but 45.6% of referrals, because many Korean searches are answered inside Naver with no click
  - Naver is a mobile engine (60.8% share on phones); Google is a desktop engine (58.6% on desktops); Daum is desktop-only
  - Naver ranks its own verticals — Blog, Cafe, Place, Knowledge iN, Shopping — with C-Rank (blog authority) and D.I.A. (per-content relevance)
  - Naver calls it a closed ecosystem (폐쇄형 생태계) — much of that content is not crawlable by Google at all
  - Commerce order on Naver: influencer reviews, long-form blog, Place, cafe, Knowledge iN, shopping listing
  - Daum fell from 20–30% at the 2014 Kakao merger to 1–3%; spun off as AXZ in Dec 2025, acquired by Upstage in May 2026
  - KakaoTalk reaches 90–98% penetration and its `#` search returns Daum results — a distribution channel, not an engine to optimize
  - Google still leads on developer/technical (~95%), English/global (~88%), video (~98%), academic (~78%) and B2B SaaS review (~70%) queries; weakest on Korean local commerce (~18%)
  - Naver's AI Briefing covered ~20% of queries by April 2026 with 30M+ MAU; Google's Korean app passed Naver's in July 2026 (47.02M vs 46.85M)

# Key Information

highlights:
  - 2025 query volume (InternetTrend): Naver 62.9%, Google 29.6%, Bing 3.1%, Daum 2.9%
  - 2025 referrals (StatCounter): Google 45.8%, Naver 45.6%, Bing 5.0%, Daum 1.2%, CocCoc 0.9%, Yandex 0.8%
  - Mobile 2025: Naver 60.8%, Google 35.9%. Desktop 2025: Google 58.6%, Naver 26.1%, Bing 10.8%, Daum 2.8%
  - The widely circulated "Google 52.4% / Naver 38.2%" pair matches neither tracker in any device split — treat it as unverified
  - Naver Search Advisor covers the external-web layer only: verification, crawl/index reports, URL inspection, reindex request
  - Knowledge iN punishes self-answered questions and sockpuppet accounts; paid cafe posting violates Korean competition rules and cafe TOS
  - Bing and Yandex both accept IndexNow for instant submission

# Summary

Korea is the one market where a local search engine genuinely beats Google, and not because Koreans dislike Google — because Naver answers a question the way a Korean person asks it: in whole sentences, about local things, assembled from Naver's own blogs, cafes, maps and Q&A. The headline numbers depend on method. On 2025 query volume (InternetTrend) Naver takes 62.9% and Google 29.6%; on 2025 outbound referrals (StatCounter) Google takes 45.8% and Naver 45.6%. Both are true. The 17-point gap is the insight: a large share of Korean searches are answered inside Naver without a click through, so a Naver strategy is not "rank higher on the same page" but "get into the vertical that answers this kind of question". Naver's verticals are VIEW (blog + cafe), Knowledge iN, Naver Blog, Cafe, Place, Shopping and News, ranked by C-Rank (blog authority and topic focus) and D.I.A. (per-content relevance, dwell time, comments). Naver itself calls this a closed ecosystem — much of that content cannot be crawled by Google at all. Device split matters: Naver is a mobile engine at 60.8%, Google a desktop engine at 58.6%, and Daum is desktop-only. Daum's own search has collapsed from 20–30% at the 2014 Kakao merger to 1–3%, but it survives as a surface inside KakaoTalk, which reaches 90–98% penetration — a distribution channel rather than an engine. Google still wins clearly on developer/technical, English, video and academic queries, and remains near half of outbound traffic. Naver's AI Briefing and Google's AI Overviews are pulling the same queries toward AI answers, which makes "being the source that gets summarized" worth more than another blue link.
