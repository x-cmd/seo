# Core Content

core_features:
  - Local engines come in three shapes: independent index (Naver, Seznam, Cốc Cốc, Yandex), licensed wrapper (Yahoo! JAPAN, Yahoo! Taiwan, Ecosia), meta-search (Startpage, MetaGer)
  - Only an independent index needs a separate SEO strategy; wrappers and meta-searches ride on your Google/Bing work
  - Market-share figures depend on the measurement basis: query volume (InternetTrend) vs outbound referral clicks (StatCounter)
  - Korea is the largest gap — 62.9% Naver on query volume vs 45.6% on referrals, because Naver answers many queries on-platform
  - Bing's index supplies Yahoo Search, DuckDuckGo and Ecosia, so one Bing submission reaches four engines
  - IndexNow covers Bing, Yandex and Seznam for instant indexing
  - "Local" only matters above ~5% of your traffic, and only if the engine has its own index and its own webmaster tool
  - A local engine usually changes four things: query language, result-page layout, submission workflow, and what a link is worth

# Key Information

highlights:
  - Worldwide 2025 (StatCounter average): Google 89.9%, Bing 4.0%, Yandex 2.2%, Yahoo! 1.4%, DuckDuckGo 0.8%, Baidu 0.7%
  - Biggest rival to Google by market, 2025 avg: Russia/Yandex 73.2%, Korea/Naver 45.6%, Taiwan/Yahoo! 13.3%, Czechia/Seznam 11.6%, Japan/Yahoo! 9.4%
  - Everything below the top five is Bing's default-setting tail — one Bing Webmaster Tools account covers it
  - Korea query volume (InternetTrend 2025): Naver 62.9%, Google 29.6%, Bing 3.1%, Daum 2.9%
  - Korea referrals (StatCounter 2025): Google 45.8%, Naver 45.6%, Bing 5.0%, Daum 1.2%
  - Privacy engine cluster in DACH + NL, 1-2% each: Ecosia, Startpage, Qwant, MetaGer — real but too small for a separate strategy
  - Naver, Seznam and Yandex each have their own webmaster tool; Yahoo! JAPAN and Yahoo! Taiwan do not, because they use Google's index

# Summary

Google handles roughly 90% of worldwide searches, so "just do Google" is right more often than not — but in about one market in ten a local engine takes a real share, and ignoring it there means leaving a third of the audience unreachable. The exceptions that matter are Russia (Yandex ~73%), Korea (Naver ~46% on referrals, ~63% on query volume), Taiwan (Yahoo! ~13%), the Czech Republic (Seznam ~12%), Japan (Yahoo! ~9%) and Vietnam (Cốc Cốc ~4%). The decisive question is not "is it local" but "does it have an index I cannot borrow". Local engines come in three shapes: independent index (Naver, Seznam, Cốc Cốc, Yandex — needs a second strategy), licensed wrapper (Yahoo! JAPAN and Yahoo! Taiwan serve Google's index, Ecosia runs on Bing's — your existing work mostly carries over), and meta-search (Startpage, MetaGer — nearly free, because you are already there). Market-share numbers must be read with their method attached: StatCounter counts outbound referral clicks, InternetTrend counts queries, and in Korea the two differ by 17 points because Naver answers many questions on its own pages without a click. Below the top five markets, the "local" engine is really just Bing's default-setting tail — and since Bing's index also feeds Yahoo, DuckDuckGo and Ecosia, one Bing Webmaster Tools account plus IndexNow covers a dozen markets. A local engine changes four practical things: query language is a question rather than a keyword, the result page is a block of the engine's own verticals rather than a list of links, submission needs that engine's own tool, and a link from a local site is worth far more than the same link from abroad.
