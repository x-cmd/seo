# Core Content

core_features:
  - Yahoo! JAPAN has used Google's search index for organic results since 2010 under a regulator-approved partnership — it is a wrapper, not a metasearch and not a competitor
  - Consequence: no separate Yahoo! JAPAN strategy or webmaster tool; Google work carries over, but titles, descriptions and click-through differ because the layout differs
  - Yahoo! JAPAN adds its own verticals on top: !-spotlight weather, news, shopping, maps, dictionaries, timetables, cloud, e-books, video, PayPay
  - Bing's Japanese share is a desktop phenomenon: 19.0% on desktop, 0.7% on mobile
  - The Mobile Software Competition Act took force in December 2025; iOS 26.2 shipped a Safari search-engine choice screen in Japan in November 2025, and Android now shows a choice screen too
  - The JFTC issued its first compliance reports and ordered Google to stop anticompetitive Android preinstall and default-search practices
  - The carrier engines (goo/KDDI-au, BIGLOBE/SoftBank, OCN/NTT Docomo) no longer have their own index — the goo portal service ended in November 2025
  - LINE Yahoo (LY Corporation) has signalled it wants to reduce dependence on Google and has tested alternative organic backends, including a possible use of another operator's index

# Key Information

highlights:
  - 2025 all-device (StatCounter): Google 79.5%, Yahoo! JAPAN 9.4%, Bing 9.2%, DuckDuckGo 0.6%, Yandex 0.6%, CocCoc 0.4%
  - 2025 mobile: Google 85.0%, Yahoo! JAPAN 12.2%, Bing 0.7%. 2025 desktop: Google 73.2%, Bing 19.0%, Yahoo! JAPAN 6.1%
  - Bing's Japanese share comes from Windows and Edge defaults, not user preference
  - Japan's mobile search share was a *setting* rather than a *choice* for years; the choice screen makes it contestable in principle
  - Real Japan-specific shift before the break: desktop Bing rose from ~17% (early 2025) to 40–52% across the first eight months of 2026 while worldwide desktop Bing FELL from ~12% to 9%
  - StatCounter's series shows a sharp global panel break in September 2026 — the Sep/Oct readings (desktop Bing near 55%) are not comparable with earlier months

# Summary

Japan looks like a two-horse race on paper — Google 79.5%, Yahoo! JAPAN 9.4%, Bing 9.2% on 2025 referral averages — but it is really a one-horse race wearing two uniforms. Yahoo! JAPAN has served organic results from Google's index since 2010 under a regulator-approved partnership that Google was even paid for, and Microsoft publicly called anticompetitive at the time. It is not a metasearch: it is Google's index wrapped in a different product, with its own interface, its own ranking layer, and its own vertical services stacked on top — !-spotlight weather, news, shopping, maps, dictionaries, timetables, cloud storage, e-books, video and the PayPay wallet. The practical consequence is good news: you need a Japanese Google strategy, not a Yahoo! JAPAN strategy, and there is no Yahoo! JAPAN webmaster tool because there is no separate index. What does need attention is presentation — titles and descriptions render differently in Yahoo! JAPAN's denser layout, so click-through rates are not the same. Bing is the desktop engine in Japan (19.0% of desktop searches against Yahoo! JAPAN's 6.1%) and is essentially absent on mobile (0.7%), because it arrives with Windows and Edge. The bigger structural change is legal: Japan's Mobile Software Competition Act took force in December 2025, iOS 26.2 shipped a Safari search-engine choice screen in November 2025, Android now shows a choice screen too, and the JFTC has ordered Google to stop anticompetitive preinstall practices — for the first time the default search engine on a Japanese phone is contestable in principle. The carrier engines (goo, BIGLOBE, OCN) are gone as separate indexes. And LINE Yahoo has begun hedging on its Google agreement, testing alternative organic backends — which, if it ever happened, would make Japan the first market where a foreign engine with its own index holds a double-digit share.
